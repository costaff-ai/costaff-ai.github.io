Room to breathe, and a floor for legibility.

A headline or another logo too close pulls the eye away, and a mark too small stops reading. A margin and a minimum size fix both.

Most of what you’d want to do is fine.

You don’t need to ask to write about CoStaff or show the logo. The things on the right aren’t forbidden; they just wear away what makes the mark recognisable.

Works well

  • Writing about CoStaffIn articles, tutorials, talks and slides, no permission needed.
  • Linking backThe logo as a link to this site.
  • Saying what you built“Built with CoStaff” or “works with CoStaff Workspace”.
  • Community materialMeetup slides, posters, community posts.
  • Scaling to fitAny size, as long as the proportions stay locked.

Weakens recognition

  • Changing the colourThe single green is the first thing people recognise.
  • Stretching or squashingThe proportions are part of what makes it readable.
  • Rotating or croppingThe lockup is drawn to sit level and whole.
  • Shadows or outlinesEffects muddy the shape, especially small.
  • Busy or same-toned backgroundsThe mark stops standing out from what is behind it.
  • Rebuilding it yourselfRetyped, the wordmark won’t match the spacing. Use the files.

The name and the code.

Each of our tools comes with its own licence. The CoStaff name and logo are not part of any of them, because they are how people tell an official release from a fork. If you ship something built on them, give it its own name and mark, and say it is built with CoStaff.
